# Quick start

If you have used a music app, you already know ultraSID. This page gets you from download to a playlist in five minutes. Everything else in the manual is background.

## 1. Install

Download ultraSID from [ultrasid.com](https://ultrasid.com) and start it. It is one file, there is no installer. On Windows, Settings, Start Menu can move it to Programs and add a Start menu entry if you prefer that.

## 2. Get the collection

The first screen asks for the High Voltage SID Collection. If you have one, point ultraSID at its `C64Music` folder or at the zip. If you don't, click "Download HVSC for me" and wait a minute. Details in [The collection](hvsc.md).

## 3. Find a tune

Start typing. The search box is where the cursor lands, and the collection narrows down with every letter: title, author, game, year. Double-click a result to play it, or press Enter.

The four buttons above the results filter what you see: Pioneers, Winners, Gems and Liked. Hover them for what they mean.

## 4. Listen

The footer works like every player: play and pause, previous and next, shuffle and repeat, a seek bar you can drag, volume. The coloured button next to the volume picks the [audio quality](audio-quality.md); Magic is the default and a good place to stay.

The right sidebar shows the STIL notes for the tune, the other subtunes in the file, and the chip at work. The CRT button in the sidebar, or a click on the thumbnail in the footer, shows the title screen or game artwork on an emulated monitor.

## 5. Make a playlist

Click the + next to "My Playlists" in the sidebar, or press Ctrl+N. Drag tunes from the search results onto it, or right-click any tune and choose "Add to playlist". Drag rows to reorder, click the title to rename, and drop an image on the page for a cover.

Click the heart on any row to like it. Alt+Shift+S shows everything you have liked.

## 6. Keys

Press `?` for the full list. The ones you will use: Space for play and pause, Ctrl+Left and Ctrl+Right for previous and next, Ctrl+F for search, F11 for the CRT in fullscreen.

That is the whole app. The rest of the manual explains why it works the way it does.
